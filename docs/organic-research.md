## 1. Executive Summary & Overview

### What is Organic Research?
**Organic Research** is the competitive and performance intelligence engine of our SEO/AEO platform. It analyzes any website's presence in non-paid (organic) search engine results pages (SERPs)—principally across Google, Bing, and hybrid AI search engines.

While paid advertising (PPC) delivers traffic only as long as you pay per click, **organic search** represents earned authority and sustainable visibility. Organic Research allows you to:
1. **Decode your own search footprint:** Discover every query your domain ranks for, where you rank, which URLs are driving traffic, and the economic value of that traffic.
2. **Reverse-engineer competitors:** Map competitor rankings, uncover their top revenue-generating content silos, identify their market weaknesses, and pinpoint high-value keyword opportunities they are capturing.
3. **Monitor algorithmic shifts:** Detect rank drops, cannibalization, and SERP feature losses before they damage business revenue.

## 2. Feature-by-Feature Deep Dive

The platform's **Organic Research** workspace is engineered around 6 analytical modules:

### Module 1: SERP Position Distribution
* **What it displays:** A segmented distribution of all ranking keywords bucketed into critical rank tiers:
  * **Positions 1–3 (Prime Conversion Zone):** Captures ~55% to 68% of all organic clicks. Highest CTR and conversion rate.
  * **Positions 4–10 (Page 1 Foundation):** Captures ~20% to 25% of clicks. These are primary candidates for quick optimization to move into Top 3.
  * **Positions 11–20 (Page 2 / "Striking Distance"):** Near zero initial clicks, but represents the highest ROI target—requiring only minor on-page or backlink tuning to cross onto Page 1.
  * **Positions 21–50 & 51–100 (Deep Index):** Long-tail or emerging relevance indicators.
* **Why it matters:** Total keyword count is a vanity metric; **position distribution** reveals true commercial power. Moving 10 keywords from Position 11 to Position 3 generates exponentially more traffic than adding 500 keywords at Position 70.

### Module 2: Search Intent Breakdown
* **What it displays:** Algorithmic categorization of all ranking keywords into 4 core user intents:
  1. **Informational (I):** Queries where users seek knowledge, guides, or definitions (e.g., *"symptoms of pediatric fever"*).
  2. **Commercial (C):** Queries where users compare products or search for reviews before buying (e.g., *"best private hospital in Phnom Penh"*).
  3. **Transactional (T):** Queries with direct purchasing intent (e.g., *"book obstetrician consultation online"*).
  4. **Navigational (N):** Brand or domain-specific lookups (e.g., *"Orienda Hospital contact number"*).
* **Why it matters:** A website with 100,000 informational visits might generate fewer conversions than a site with 5,000 transactional visits. Intent mapping ensures content matches what Google's ranking algorithms expect for each query type.

### Module 3: SERP Features in Search Landscape
* **What it displays:** Rich search result enhancements won or lost by the domain:
  * **Featured Snippets (Position 0):** Direct answer boxes at the very top of SERPs.
  * **People Also Ask (PAA):** Expandable FAQ accordions.
  * **Local Pack (Map 3-Pack):** Google Maps local business integration.
  * **Knowledge Panels:** Google Knowledge Graph entity representations.
  * **SiteLinks & Image/Video Packs:** Expanded visual real estate.
* **Why it matters:** Modern search is not just 10 blue links. Winning Featured Snippets and PAA accordions pushes traditional competitors below the fold and feeds directly into **AI Overviews** and LLM answer systems.

### Module 4: Organic Competitors Positioning Matrix
* **What it displays:**
  * Direct SERP rivals based on shared search terms.
  * **Common Keywords:** Exact volume of keywords where both your domain and the competitor rank in the top 100.
  * **Search Visibility Score:** Aggregate impression share based on CTR curves.
  * **Estimated Organic Traffic:** Projected monthly visits calculated from ranking positions multiplied by search volume.
* **Why it matters:** Your true organic competitors in Google are often completely different from your offline business competitors. For instance, a private clinic may compete offline with another clinic, but compete on Google with government health portals, Wikipedia, and medical directories.

### Module 5: Top Organic Pages & Content Silos
* **What it displays:** URL-level breakdown of traffic drivers across the domain:
  * Traffic share percentage per URL.
  * Total ranking keywords per page.
  * Top primary driver keyword and its current SERP rank.
* **Why it matters:** The 80/20 rule applies to SEO: typically 20% of pages generate 80% of organic traffic. Identifying these pillar pages allows you to protect them against regressions, optimize internal links to bolster weaker pages, and identify underperforming content silos.

### Module 6: Keyword Rankings & Historical SERP Positions
* **What it displays:** The granular search terms driving visibility:
  * Keyword phrase, search volume (monthly searches in target country/language).
  * Current Position and Position Delta (+/- change).
  * Cost-per-Click (CPC) benchmark: what advertisers pay in Google Ads for this click.
  * Keyword Difficulty (KD %): Competitive intensity to rank.
  * Landing page URL.
* **Why it matters:** Provides the tactical execution worklist for content rewrites, meta tag optimization, and link acquisition.

## 4. Why We Use Organic Research (Strategic Value)

1. **Compounding ROI vs. Paid Ads:**
   * Paid search traffic ceases the moment ad spend stops.
   * Organic rankings continue delivering qualified leads month after month with zero marginal cost per click.
2. **Identifying "Striking Distance" Opportunities:**
   * Keywords ranking in positions 4 through 15 are already indexed and trusted by Google.
   * Applying targeted on-page fixes (e.g., adding structured schema, optimizing H2 headers, improving page speed) routinely boosts these terms into the Top 3 within 2–4 weeks.
3. **Preventing Keyword Cannibalization:**
   * Organic Research reveals when two different URLs on your site are competing for the same search phrase, confusing Google and depressing rankings for both.
4. **Competitor Content Heist:**
   * By examining the top pages of leading competitors, you discover content topics and structural layouts that Google has already proven it prefers to reward.

## 5. How Organic Research Directly Helps SEO

* **Keyword Discovery & Expansion:** Reveals long-tail variations and commercial queries that target customers actually type.
* **Content Optimization & Intent Alignment:** Ensures informational pages don't attempt to hard-sell, and transactional product pages don't bury the call-to-action under 2,000 words of background info.
* **SERP Feature Hijacking:** Provides exact phrasing needed to win Featured Snippets and People Also Ask boxes.
* **Direct Bridge to AEO (Answer Engine Optimization):** Pages ranking in Google's Top 3 with clear heading structures and concise summaries are **3.8x more likely** to be cited by Perplexity, ChatGPT Search, and Google AI Overviews.

## 6. Step-by-Step SOP: Executing an Organic Research Audit

1. **Step 1: Domain Ingestion & SERP Scan**
   Select or scan the target client domain in the dashboard.
2. **Step 2: Position Distribution Review**
   Open Organic Research and review the Position Distribution. Identify keywords in Positions 4–10 and 11–20.
3. **Step 3: Intent Segmentation**
   Filter by Commercial & Transactional Intent to focus on revenue-generating terms.
4. **Step 4: Competitor Overlap Analysis**
   Review the Competitor Matrix and click Gap → to inspect missing keywords in Keyword Gap.
5. **Step 5: Route to Auto-Fix Code Engine**
   Send high-opportunity terms to the Auto-Fix Code Engine or Plan & Triage for autonomous remediation.
